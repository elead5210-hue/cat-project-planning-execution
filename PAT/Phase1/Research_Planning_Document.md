## 2. Focus question

> **How is modern technology helping to reduce the effects of climate change, and which solutions are the most effective?**

The question has two parts. The first asks *how* technology helps. The second asks *which* solutions work best, so I must compare the technologies and give reasons for my conclusion.

6. How can eco-friendly apps help ordinary people to live in a more sustainable way?

## 4. Suitable information sources

A good source is reliable, up to date, relevant and written for a suitable audience. I will prefer sources that are shown below.

| Type of source | Examples | Why it is suitable |
|---|---|---|
| International organisations | United Nations (UN), Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), International Energy Agency (IEA) | Their reports are researched by experts and based on evidence. |
| Government and science bodies | NASA, national environmental departments, national energy research laboratories | They are public bodies that publish facts and data. |
| Specialist organisations | Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) for farming, environmental agencies for recycling | They focus on one field and know it well. |
| Educational websites | Encyclopaedias, school and university websites | They explain ideas clearly for learners. |
| News and magazine articles | Reputable science and technology news sites | They are useful for recent developments, but I will check their facts against another source. |
| Primary source | My own questionnaire | It gives me first-hand data on what learners think. |

**How I will judge a source:**

- **Author or organisation:** Who wrote it, and are they an expert?
- **Date:** Is it recent enough for a fast-changing topic?
- **Purpose:** Is it trying to inform, or to sell something?
- **Evidence:** Does it give data, and can I confirm it in a second source?

**Sources I will avoid:** blogs with no named author, sites that sell products, and pages with no date or no clear author.

## 5. Sifted and summarised information

*The notes below are in my own words. Figures should be checked against the sources in the reference list in section 6 before they are used in Phase 2.*

### 5.1 Background: climate change

Climate change is a long-term change in the Earth's temperatures and weather patterns. Today it is driven mainly by human activity. Burning fossil fuels such as coal, oil and gas releases greenhouse gases, mainly carbon dioxide, which trap heat in the atmosphere. This leads to a warming planet, rising sea levels, and more extreme weather such as droughts, floods and heatwaves. Reducing these effects means cutting emissions and using resources more carefully.

### 5.2 Solar power

- Solar panels (photovoltaic panels) turn sunlight directly into electricity.
- They produce no greenhouse gas emissions while generating power, so they replace electricity that would otherwise come from burning coal or gas.
- They can be used on a large scale (solar farms) or on the roofs of homes, schools and businesses.
- **Limitations:** They only generate power when the sun shines, so storage batteries or other power sources are needed. They also have a manufacturing cost and need space.

### 5.3 Wind power

- Wind turbines use moving air to turn blades connected to a generator, which makes electricity.
- Like solar, they produce no emissions while running.
- They work best in places with steady wind, either on land or at sea.
- **Limitations:** They depend on the wind, can affect the landscape and wildlife, and need a strong electricity network to carry the power.

### 5.4 Electric vehicles (EVs)

- Electric vehicles run on batteries instead of petrol or diesel, so they produce no exhaust emissions from the tailpipe. This improves air quality in cities.
- Over their whole life, they usually cause fewer emissions than petrol cars, especially when the electricity they use comes from renewable sources.
- **Limitations:** They cost more to buy, need charging points, and making the batteries uses mining and energy. If the electricity comes from coal, the benefit is smaller.

### 5.5 Smart farming

- Smart farming (precision agriculture) uses technology such as sensors, drones, GPS and data analysis to manage crops and animals.
- Farmers can give water, fertiliser and pesticides only where and when they are needed. This saves water, reduces waste, lowers pollution and can increase harvests.
- **Limitations:** The equipment and training can be expensive, and small farmers may find it hard to afford.

### 5.6 Recycling technologies

- Recycling technologies sort and process waste so that materials such as plastic, glass, metal, paper and electronics can be used again.
- Reusing materials reduces the energy needed to make new products from raw materials and keeps waste out of landfills, where rotting waste can release methane, a strong greenhouse gas.
- Modern systems use automatic sorting machines, sensors and new methods to recycle materials that were once hard to recycle.
- **Limitations:** Recycling works only if waste is collected and sorted properly, and some materials remain difficult to recycle.

### 5.7 Eco-friendly apps

- Eco-friendly apps help people make greener choices. They can track a person's carbon footprint, remind users to recycle, help them find public transport or charging points, reduce food waste, or show how much energy a home uses.
- They are cheap, easy to access on a phone and can change everyday habits.
- **Limitations:** An app only helps if people actually use it, and its impact on its own is small compared with large-scale technologies such as solar and wind power.

### 5.8 Summary of comparison

| Technology | Main way it helps | Scale of impact | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar power | Clean electricity from sunlight | Large | Needs storage when there is no sun |
| Wind power | Clean electricity from wind | Large | Depends on wind and location |
| Electric vehicles | No tailpipe emissions | Medium to large | Cost and battery production |
| Smart farming | Saves water, fertiliser and land | Medium | Equipment cost |
| Recycling technologies | Saves resources and reduces waste | Medium | Needs good collection and sorting |
| Eco-friendly apps | Encourage greener daily habits | Small to medium | Depends on people using them |

**Initial conclusion (to be tested in Phase 2):** Technologies that replace fossil fuels in electricity generation, such as solar and wind power, appear to be the most effective because they affect emissions on the largest scale. The other solutions are important too, and they work best when used together. I will compare this view with what learners say in my questionnaire.
